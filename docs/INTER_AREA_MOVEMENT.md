# Moving between areas

Creatures in the original route through doors and transition triggers to an object or location in another area, and
an attacker follows a target that leaves through one. This page describes how the original behaves, measured on the
dedicated server (`nwserver`) and the game client (`nwmain`), and how OpenWinter reproduces it.

- Code: `src/OpenWinter.Game/WorldObjects/InterAreaMovement.cs` (`AreaRoutes`, the route planner;
  `InterAreaMoveAction`, the move), `TransitionService.NpcDestination` / `Destination` / `BringAssociates` /
  `OnMoved` (keyboard into a doorway), `World.Whereabouts` (a loading player's area), the pursuit in
  `Combat/AttackAction.cs`, the script functions in `Scripting/Functions/CommandFunctions.cs`, killed doors in
  `WorldObjects/DoorService.cs`.
- Tests: `tests/OpenWinter.Tests/InterAreaMovementTests.cs`, `BashedDoorTests.cs`,
  `PlayerTransitionFollowerTests.cs`.

## Test areas

The server suites use flat 30 x 30 m areas of open `tde01` floor, generated by `parity/gen_interarea.py` (see
[parity/README.md](../parity/README.md)). Door pairs are two stock generic doors (`nw_door_normal`) whose LinkedTo
names each other; trigger pairs are 4 x 2 m `newtransition` triggers leading to a waypoint 2.5 m inside the other
area. Walkers are `nw_bandit001` copies without AI scripts at AI_LEVEL_NORMAL unless stated. Area, door and trigger
events log the creature, the time and the place. The player-side behaviour was measured on `nwmain` in the Prelude's
Graduation Chamber and Training Halls, joined by the doorway `M0Q1C_M0Q1B` / `M0Q1B_M0Q1C` (a `T_DOOR01` door).

## Moves to another area

`ActionMoveToObject`, `ActionForceMoveToObject` (and `ActionMoveToLocation`, `ActionForceMoveToLocation`) to a target
in another area route through transitions; `GetCurrentAction` reads `ACTION_MOVETOPOINT` (0) throughout.

- **Doors and triggers** both work, any number of areas deep (measured up to four).
- **Route**: the fewest areas. Given a two-area route through one 80 m area and a three-area route through two
  20 m ones, the walker takes the long two-area route. A dead-end branch is ignored. Of two routes through as many
  areas, the one whose exit in the first area lies further west (lower X) is taken, however long the walk. Routes
  are searched depth first up to 9 transitions (`AreaRoutes.Plan`).
- **Locked and closed doors are planned through as if open.** A locked door's one-area route beats an unlocked
  two-area one. At a closed door the walker stands at the door's use point and OnBlocked fires every tick (about
  every 0.05 s); the move never fails. If OnBlocked opens the door (the default `nw_c2_defaulte`'s `DoDoorAction`),
  the open runs first (the door opens about 1.1 s after the block) and the walk goes on. A locked door stops it for
  good; the stock bandit bashes it down (about 37 s) and then goes through.
- **No route** (target in an unconnected area, or a door whose LinkedTo names nothing): an object move stands where
  it is, still queued, reading 0 for about a second and `ACTION_WAIT` (36) for the next, over and over. A location
  move ends at once.
- **SetTransitionTarget** on a trigger without a link is used like a link.
- **Where the door is taken**: the walker stops where `ActionOpenDoor` would (1.05 m short of the door's closed-state
  use point, on the line it comes in on) and goes through without stopping in the area it set out from; after its
  first transition it brakes and stands about 0.4 s at each further door.
- **Triggers** are taken by walking into them (the trigger's OnEnter fires). A walk across a transition trigger that
  isn't part of a route (a same-area `ActionMoveToLocation`) does not take it.
- **OnClick**: a door's or trigger's OnClick script runs for an NPC (with `GetClickingObject` = the NPC, whose
  `GetCurrentAction` reads `ACTION_INVALID` inside it) instead of the transition, again every 0.67 s while the script
  doesn't move it.
- **Area events**: the old area's OnExit, then the new area's OnEnter; inside OnEnter, and for a moment after
  arriving, `GetCurrentAction` reads `ACTION_INVALID` (65535).
- **Landing**:
  - through a door, on the destination door's outer side: (0.4, -1.9) m in the door's frame, which is its walkmesh's
    `dp_closed_01` use point 1 m further out, whichever way the door faces and whichever side the walker approached
    from;
  - through a trigger, on the target waypoint;
  - in both cases moved clear of creatures standing there (the usual personal-space search).
- **Pause**: the creature stands about 1.3 s after arriving (1.25 to 1.33 s measured, in intermediate and final
  areas alike), then goes on (`ArrivalPause`).
- **Its queue carries on**: actions queued after the move (an `ActionDoCommand`) run when it reaches the target.
- **After a trigger the route is forgotten.** The walker first walks towards the target's coordinates in the area it
  has just entered (stopping 1 m short; if they're off that area's ground, as far as it gets), stands about 1 s, then
  plans again. In the target's own area it walks straight onto the target.
- **After a door** the walk goes on to the next transition.
- **In the target's area**:
  - a move whose first route was a single door first walks to the coordinates of the door it came through (that
    door's use point, 1 m short), then to the target, stopping at the usual range (1 m from a waypoint). A stock
    bandit returning to its post shows this: it walks past its post towards the door's coordinates, then back;
  - a longer route ends exactly on the target.
- **Running**: when `bRun` is TRUE the walker runs in the area it sets out from and on routes it plans on the way.
  It walks:
  - the leg through the area its first door led into;
  - the detours after triggers;
  - the last leg to the target.
- **Location moves go only part of the way.** `ActionMoveToLocation` to a location three areas away takes the first
  transition, then:
  - after a door, it walks to the next transition and ends there (without taking it);
  - after a trigger, it walks to the location's coordinates in that area and ends there.
- **ActionMoveAwayFromObject** from an object in another area ends at once without moving. In its own area it sets
  off after 0.37 s and walks 10 or 30 m without another stand.
- **ActionForceMoveToObject** routes like ActionMoveToObject; when its time runs out it jumps to the target.

### Moves whose target is a transition

- **ActionMoveToObject(open transition door)** takes the mover through on arrival, a player and an NPC alike. With a
  3 m range the mover stops short and stays; at a closed door it stops at the use point and the door stays shut.
  OpenWinter's `MoveAction.TakeDoorTransition` (set by ActionMoveToObject) takes the door when the move arrives with
  the default range (1 m or less); an NPC lands where an NPC route does (`TransitionService.NpcDestination`).
- **Transition triggers take only a player who walks in herself.** Walking into the trigger by keyboard takes her
  through; a scripted ActionMoveToLocation onto it, or through it to a point beyond, walks on and stays in the area
  (`TransitionService.OnTriggerEntered`).
- **ActionMoveToObject(transition trigger) for an NPC**: without an OnClick script it walks to the trigger's outline
  and is taken through; with one it stops on the outline, OnClick runs with it as the clicking object, and it stays.
- **ActionMoveToObject(ordinary trigger with OnClick)** is used only from inside: a walker inside clicks at once
  without moving; one outside stops at the usual range from the trigger's position (0.30, 1.00, 5.00 m for fRange
  0.1, 1 and 5) and stands there in its move action, never clicking. Without OnClick it stops 0.88-0.9 m from the
  outline, from either side.
- **A trap trigger** is approached at a point 0.5 m outside its nearest outline point; the walker stops 0.67 m out and
  stays in its move action.
- **A closed transition door** is not opened by such a move: the walker stands 1.14 m from its use point in its move
  action. A mover that holds never gives up (still holding 45 s after the order).

Code: `MoveAction`, `TriggerUse`.

## Pursuit

An attacker whose target goes to another area follows it through the transitions:
- through doors and triggers, and through further areas;
- with or without the default AI scripts;
- whether or not it saw the target go (a target jumped two areas away, or in another area from the start, never
  seen, is followed);
- however far behind it is (an attacker 38 m away from the door followed).

This is the attack action itself (`ActionAttack`), not the AI scripts. Only an attack ordered with
`ActionAttack(o, FALSE)` (the default) follows; one ordered with `ActionAttack(o, TRUE)` doesn't (suite
`attack_leave`):
- A passive attack whose target jumped to another area ends in the next update (GetCurrentAction ACTION_INVALID about
  0.27 s after the jump), or, with a hostile creature 1.5 m from the attacker, goes on against it (its first swing
  1.6 s later). The explicit attack, with or without that creature beside it, follows the target.
- A target jumped to an area with no route to it: the NPC keeps its attack action (ACTION_ATTACKOBJECT, no attack
  target) and stands where it is, for at least 19 s, even with a hostile creature beside it.
- Encounter creatures chase a target in the middle of a transition (a player loading the next area) like any NPC.
- A player's own attack ends in the update after its target jumps away (she stands still), or, with a hostile
  creature 1.4 m from her, the attack target clears at once and she takes that creature up at her next swing (1.8 s
  later): `AttackAction`.
- While it chases in its own area it reads `ACTION_ATTACKOBJECT` (3) and runs; after the first transition it reads 0
  and walks.
- It arrives about 1.5 s after a target it was right behind.
- At each further door it stands about 0.9 s (reading 3) before going through.
- In the target's area it walks to where the target was, then fights on.
- No `OnEndCombatRound` fires while it is on its way.
- It does not come back by itself; the stock scripts' post walking (below) is what brings a bandit home.
- A pursuer is never given an attack of opportunity by the target it follows (it is walking towards it, not away).

## Associates

A creature's summoned creature jumps with it at the transition, in the same instant, 1 m from it (towards the door it
came through). This was measured with an NPC master. A player's associates are covered under [Players](#players).

## Players

- **Walking into a doorway by keyboard.** Walking into an open doorway takes it: the player goes through when its
  centre reaches the door's line. At the same doorway closed it stops 0.27 m short and the door stays shut.
  OpenWinter takes an open (or killed) transition door when a keyboard-steered player's step reaches its line (within
  0.25 m, moving towards it) or crosses it within the closed leaf's width (`TransitionService.OnMoved`); a closed door
  only stops it. Transition triggers are taken by walking into them.
- **Out of every area while loading.** Between leaving and appearing, GetArea on the player reads invalid (about
  0.3 s in the original). OpenWinter does the same (the area-ready handshake). Pursuit and `BringAssociates` go by
  `World.Whereabouts`, which for a loading player is the area it is loading and the spot it will stand on.
- **Landing through a door**: (0.37, -1.70) in the destination door's frame, 0.8 m past its closed-state use point
  (T_DOOR01's `dp_closed_01` is (0.37, -0.9)), facing straight away from the door's centre, whichever way the door
  faces; the same for JumpToObject to the door (`TransitionService.Destination`). A creature jumped to a door lands
  where its route would put it (`NpcDestination`).
- **The attacker follows.** A bandit attacking the player (`ActionAttack`, stock scripts) walks to the doorway
  reading `ACTION_ATTACKOBJECT` while the player loads, goes through about 0.5 s after the player (from about 2 m
  short of the door's line, as an NPC route takes a door), lands 1 m from the player, stands about 1.3 s and walks on
  after it reading `ACTION_MOVETOPOINT`. GetAttackTarget reads invalid from the moment the player sets off.
- **Associates come.** A henchman (AddHenchman), the familiar (SummonFamiliar), a summoned creature
  (EffectSummonCreature) and a dominated creature (EffectDominated) appear in the new area in the same instant as
  the player, 0.6 to 1.1 m round its spot (moved clear of it), however far away they were (a summon 14 m and a
  henchman 24 m away came the same way). It is the same through a doorway by keyboard, by JumpToObject and by a
  cross-area ActionMoveToObject. OpenWinter brings a player's associates on any change of area
  (`World.CreatureLeftArea`), once the player appears.

### A player's own associates make way

A player walking to a doorway past her own summoned creature is never blocked by it: an idle summon in her path is
pushed 1 m aside as she reaches it, and a walking summon crossing her path goes round her. OpenWinter
(`PersonalSpace`):
- creatures nobody plays never push a player, plan their paths round her even when she is idle, and plan again round
  her when she steps into their way instead of giving the move up;
- a player's path ignores her own associates; one on the move makes way (it re-plans round her) and a standing one is
  pushed aside even when busy.

### Loading the next area (client)

The original has a player out of every area for about 0.3 s. OpenWinter's client answers area-ready the frame after
the area has drawn under the loader (`ClientSession.CompleteAfterFrame`), and the keys work from then on; the loader
(at least 0.3 s, `LoadingScreen.AreaMinimum`) fades off over the live area. A player arriving with a movement key
held walks on at once, as in the original; one turning straight back into the doorway gets the loader back opaque.

To keep loads short:
- collision shapes (camera collision and tile fading) are made from the model builder's own triangles, once per
  model, and an area's tiles and placeables share their model's meshes (`ModelBuilder.BuildStatic(shareMeshes)`).
  Shared meshes and shapes that none of the last three areas used are released;
- sound objects keep their sounds' names and load them on a background thread (`AudioLoader.Preload`), playing each
  once it is ready;
- emitters share their chunk mesh and particle quads (`AuroraEmitter.WithMaterial`, `DrawQuad`).

`[client] area <resref> ...` in the log gives each load's wall-clock marks (`AreaLoadTiming`): the build (tiles,
border), the objects (`held`, with each kind of object's total), the server's snapshot and `ready` (area-ready sent).
Typical loads: a small room 0.14 to 0.4 s, a 16x16 exterior 0.25 to 0.75 s (first visits are slower: tile meshes,
doors, the player's creature and Godot compiling new material pipelines).

## Stock AI walking back to its post

`x0_i0_walkway`'s `WalkWayPoints` remembers the `POST_<tag>` waypoint when the creature spawns. Its default heartbeat
calls `WalkWayPoints` again, which does an `ActionMoveToObject` to the post, and that crosses areas as above.
- A bandit with the stock `nw_c2_default*` scripts, jumped one, two or three areas from its post (doors, and
  door-trigger-door), walks home on its next heartbeat at AI_LEVEL_NORMAL and stops on the post.
- At the default AI level in an area without players (`AI_LEVEL_VERY_LOW`) the stock heartbeat returns at once, so it
  never goes home. Scripted moves still cross areas at that level.
- A locked door on the way home is bashed down by the stock OnBlocked script, then taken.

## Killed doors

A door killed by damage, by EffectDeath or by bashing still leads on. Code: `DoorService.Break`
(`WorldObjects/DoorService.cs`), `Door.Broken`.
- **What is left.** The killed door stays the same object: its locals, its tag, its position and its area.
  - It is open at once, in the same script, without OnOpen. Its lock stays as it was.
  - Damage leaves its hit points below zero, and GetIsDead reads TRUE. EffectDeath leaves its hit points, and
    GetIsDead reads FALSE.
  - Nothing else appears where it stood.
- **Events.** OnDamaged, then the linked door's OnDeath, then its own OnDeath, all in the same moment.
- **The linked door breaks too.** The door its transition leads to opens with it, without OnOpen, runs its own
  OnDeath and is drawn gone like the killed door, but keeps its hit points and does not read dead (like a door killed
  by EffectDeath).
- **Doors without a transition** (no LinkedTo, or one that names nothing) are removed 2.0 to 2.1 s after their
  death. A door whose transition leads to a door or a waypoint stays for good.
- **Still a transition, both ways.**
  - Creatures route and pursue through it as through an open door, and land at the same point outside the far door.
  - A creature coming back through the linked door lands outside the killed door, where JumpToObject to it lands
    too.
  - GetTransitionTarget is unchanged on both doors.
  - An OnClick (OnAreaTransitionClick) script still runs instead of the transition.
  - Walking across the doorway inside its area does not transition.
- **No walkmesh.** Walkers cross the doorway in a straight line and pass where the leaf stood. A live door's open
  leaf makes them step 1 m round it.
- **Door actions.**
  - The killed door does nothing by itself: its own ActionCloseDoor and ActionOpenDoor do nothing.
  - A player told to ActionInteractObject it does nothing either.
  - SetLocked works and runs OnLock or OnUnlock.
  - A creature can close it again (OnClosed; the linked door closes too). It still has no walkmesh, but a route
    through it stops there as at any closed door, and the walker's DoDoorAction(DOOR_ACTION_OPEN) opens it (OnOpen).
  - GetIsDoorActionPossible: OPEN as for any door, UNLOCK and KNOCK FALSE, BASH TRUE.
- **Closed and locked again**, it traps a route walker for good in the original: a creature's
  DoDoorAction(DOOR_ACTION_BASH) does not even start an attack. OpenWinter differs on purpose so that a killed
  transition door never traps anyone: an attack on a closed killed door knocks it open again (`DoorService.KnockOpen`).
- **DestroyObject** does not remove a door with a transition. The door opens instead, without OnOpen, and its linked
  door stays shut until a creature comes through, when it opens itself (OnOpen, opened by itself). DestroyObject on
  any other door works as usual.
- **Related door behaviour:**
  - JumpToObject to any door lands where a creature coming through it would.
  - DoDoorAction(DOOR_ACTION_OPEN) on a door that already stands open runs OnOpen on arrival, without the second of
    reaching for it; ActionOpenDoor beside an open door still takes 1 s.
  - A creature attacking a door faced head-on closes in on the door itself.
  - GetIsDead on an object that no longer exists reads TRUE.
  - A door that opens itself swings its leaf to side 2; "open 1" is only for an opening creature on the side the
    door faces (its GetFacing, the bearing plus 90 degrees), so the leaf swings away from it. A linked door takes the
    same side.

**What the player sees.** The door model plays its `die` animation: the leaf falls in a cloud of dust, by 1.5 s it is
gone, and then the dust clears from an empty doorway. The killed transition door, its linked door and an ordinary
door all look the same; the ordinary one is then removed. OpenWinter sends the dead flag for a killed door
(`GameServer` spawn flags), plays `die` and holds the `dead` pose.

**What the player does.** A killed door counts as open. Hovering the doorway shows the transition plane and cursor,
and a click takes the transition (`TransitionService.Click`). Doors are not taken by walking through them: walkers
crossing a killed door's doorway stay in the area.

## Testing

```
dotnet run --project src/OpenWinter.Tools -c Release -- parity-run interarea_move
dotnet run --project src/OpenWinter.Tools -c Release -- parity-run interarea_pursuit
dotnet run --project src/OpenWinter.Tools -c Release -- parity-run attack_leave
dotnet run --project src/OpenWinter.Tools -c Release -- parity-run door_bashed
python tools/visual/visual.py run transitfollow
```

- `interarea_move` and `interarea_pursuit`: routing, landing, pauses and pursuit across door and trigger chains.
- `attack_leave`: passive and explicit attacks on a target that leaves, and an unreachable area.
- `door_bashed`: killed doors (EffectDamage, EffectDeath, a bandit's `DoDoorAction(DOOR_ACTION_BASH)`, DestroyObject).
- Other server suites: `approach_click` (moves whose target is a trigger), `route_arrival`, `route_ties`,
  `moveaway_legs`, `door_leafside`.
- Visual scenes on `nwmain`: `transitfollow` (keyboard doorway, followers, associates), `doormove` / `doormove2`
  (scripted moves onto a transition), `summondoor` (associates making way), `doorbash` (killed door animation),
  `areaload` (load times).

## Not matched yet

- **Players**:
  - a first visit to a large area can take OpenWinter's client up to about 0.75 s, so an attacker close behind can
    land first there;
  - in OpenWinter EffectDominated from a script doesn't make the creature an associate (only Animal Empathy does),
    and SummonAnimalCompanion summons one without the feat;
  - ActionMoveToObject(trigger) leaves the original's player where she stands; OpenWinter's walks to the trigger's
    edge (as both engines' NPCs do);
  - ActionMoveToObject(open transition door) stops 1 m short of the use point where the original walks onto it, and
    3 m short with a 3 m range (1.9 m in the original);
  - a player's ground click through a transition trigger still takes it (the original's was not measured);
  - not measured: the animal companion; associates already in another area; NPC masters jumped by script; a player
    who can't reach her target in her own area.
- **A pursuer in the target's new area** in the original walks on past the target to 1 m short of where the door it
  took stood in the old area, and only then turns back and fights (13.5 s after arriving). OpenWinter's walks to the
  target and fights 8 s after arriving.
- **Killed doors**:
  - a player going through one in the original is not measured. OpenWinter treats it as the open transition door it
    is;
  - after its partner's death the linked door reads GetIsDoorActionPossible(DOOR_ACTION_BASH) FALSE in the original,
    though an open door that nothing killed reads TRUE. Not modelled;
  - after bashing the door the original's basher walks onto its goal, where OpenWinter stops 1 m short, as after any
    one-door route;
  - a closed and locked killed door is knocked open by a bash in OpenWinter, not in the original (above);
  - in OpenWinter the leaf is already hidden 0.3 s into `die`, where the original still shows it falling;
  - a creature closing a door finishes 0.5 to 0.9 s sooner in OpenWinter;
  - not measured: doors whose only transition is SetTransitionTarget, one-way links, placeables.
- **Walking round open leaves**: OpenWinter's walker can still get stuck walking round a live door's open leaf where
  the original's goes round it.
- **Timing**: later legs drift by up to about 1.4 s, and the suites widen their tolerances by 0.4 s per area crossed.
  Two causes:
  - when a door opens itself, the original chooses the side its leaf swings to, so a leaf sometimes stands in the
    walker's way;
  - the original turns on the spot more slowly than OpenWinter (there is no wait before a move sets off, turning or
    not; only moving away waits 0.3 s).
- **Pursuit after a fight in the first area**: OpenWinter's attacker reacts to the target moving away about half a
  second sooner, so on the far side it lands in a slightly different spot and can bump into the target sooner.
- **Where a walker enters a trigger** from the area it set out from differs by about 0.4 m.
- **The original's quirks that are not copied**:
  - after a ForceMove timeout jump it walks on towards its old leg's coordinates;
  - two running walkers can stand forever at the arrival point of a door whose next transition is a trigger into the
    final area.
- **Not measured**: a closed door in the target's own area; a transition inside one area; doors whose destination is
  a waypoint; encounter creatures on server routes.
